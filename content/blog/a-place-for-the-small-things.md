A message to answer. A detail to check. An idea that arrives halfway through
something else. Small things add up. Writing them down gives you a place to
start.

## Start by writing it down

A useful note does not need to be polished. It only needs to make sense when you
come back to it.

Capture enough context to recognise the task later: what needs doing, who it
concerns, and anything you would otherwise have to find again. A few extra words
now can save a second round of remembering.

## Give it a clear next step

“Website” is a subject. “Review the homepage introduction” is something you can
do. The more clearly a task names its next step, the easier it is to begin.

- What would moving this forward look like?
- Is there one small action I can name?
- Does this need a date, or simply a place to wait?

Not every task needs a deadline. Sometimes it is enough to know that you have
written it down and can return to it.

section always starts below it, so nothing ever sits beside a heading.

## Leave room to come back

A list is useful when it reflects the work you actually intend to do. That means
returning to it, changing a few words, and letting go of things that no longer
matter.

Keep the review small. Choose one thing to move forward, put the rest in order,
and get back to your day.
